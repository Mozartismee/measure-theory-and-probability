# Corrigé — $L^p$ Interfaces and RN Consumers

首次完整作答以前不要讀本檔。Canonical assignments 的完整證明以正式 corrigé 為正本；本檔只核對 probability-space synthesis 與 July consumer map。

## Exercise 0

For $1\le p<+\infty$,

$$
L^p(\mathbb P)
=
\left\{
X:\Omega\to\mathbb R\text{ measurable}:
\mathbb E[|X|^p]<+\infty
\right\}\big/\!\sim_{\mathbb P},
$$

where $X\sim_{\mathbb P}Y$ if and only if $X=Y$ $\mathbb P$-almost surely. The ambient measure determines integrability, the norm, the null sets and hence the equivalence classes.

On a finite measure space, if $1\le p<q<+\infty$, Hölder gives

$$
\|X\|_p
\le
\mu(E)^{1/p-1/q}\|X\|_q.
$$

The endpoint is

$$
\|X\|_p
\le
\mu(E)^{1/p}\|X\|_\infty.
$$

Since $\mathbb P(\Omega)=1$, all constants reduce to one. Therefore

$$
\|X\|_1
\le
\|X\|_2
\le
\|X\|_\infty,
\qquad
L^\infty(\mathbb P)
\subseteq
L^2(\mathbb P)
\subseteq
L^1(\mathbb P).
$$

Moreover,

$$
X\in L^p(\mathbb P)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\mathbb E[|X|^p]<+\infty.
$$

The two product interfaces are

$$
\|YZ\|_1
\le
\|Y\|_1\|Z\|_\infty
$$

for $Y\in L^1$ and $Z\in L^\infty$, and

$$
\|UV\|_1
\le
\|U\|_2\|V\|_2
$$

for $U,V\in L^2$. The first is the $L^1$--$L^\infty$ endpoint of Hölder; the second is Cauchy--Schwarz.

Thus $L^1$ controls finite expectations and finite signed RN numerators, $L^\infty$ controls indicators and bounded tests, and $L^2$ controls second moments and integrable cross terms.

The first row is the generic forward density construction. The event-conditioning row also has an explicit forward density,

$$
\frac{d\mathbb P_B}{d\mathbb P}
=
\frac{\mathbf1_B}{\mathbb P(B)}.
$$

The probability-density row and the conditional-expectation row use an RN converse after absolute continuity and the appropriate theorem regime have been checked.

The law

$$
\mu_X=X_\#\mathbb P
$$

is already a measure on the state space. It is not a density until a reference measure has been chosen and $\mu_X$ has been proved absolutely continuous with respect to it.

For a signed conditional-expectation numerator, write its Jordan decomposition and apply the positive RN theorem to the positive and negative parts. Their derivatives may then be subtracted; finiteness prevents an undefined $+\infty-\infty$. The result is unique $\mathbb P$-almost surely on $(\Omega,\mathcal G)$.

## Closed-book chain

The legitimate chain is

$$
\boxed{Y\in L^q(\mathbb P),\ q>1}
\Longrightarrow
\boxed{Y\in L^1(\mathbb P)}
\Longrightarrow
\boxed{Y\mathbb P\text{ is a finite signed measure}}.
$$

An RN representation is available only after a reference measure and absolute continuity have been specified. The $L^p$ embedding does not imply Hahn local domination.
