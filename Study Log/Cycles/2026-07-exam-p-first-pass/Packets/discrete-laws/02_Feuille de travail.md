## Entry Gate — Fifteen-minute recognition test

No proof longer than four lines is requested in this gate.

1. Let $E$ be countable and let $\mu$ be a probability measure on $(E,2^E)$. Define the function $p$ which represents $\mu$ with respect to counting measure, state the representation of $\mu(A)$, and give the exact uniqueness basis.
2. Let $\#$ be counting measure on the uncountable Borel space $[0,1]$. State why Lebesgue measure $\lambda$ satisfies $\lambda\ll\#$ and why no measurable $f$ can satisfy $\lambda=f\#$.
3. Let $Y=g(X)$. Write the legitimate formula for $\mathbb E[Y]$ in terms of the law of $X$, and state the invalid interchange which it does not imply.
4. Let $X,Y$ be integer-valued. Write the law of $X+Y$ first in terms of the joint pmf, then state the additional hypothesis which turns the formula into convolution of the marginals.
5. Match each verbal construction with a law or class of laws:
   - a fixed number of iid Bernoulli trials;
   - a uniform sample without replacement from a finite population;
   - the trial on which the $r$-th success occurs;
   - a Poisson count with a non-degenerate square-integrable random intensity.

If one of the four objects—density, pushforward, joint law or construction—cannot be named, record it before reading.

## Exercise A — Identical marginals, different pushforwards

Let $B\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(1/2)$. Define

$$
(X_+,Y_+):=(B,B),
\qquad
(X_-,Y_-):=(B,1-B).
$$

1. Prove that all four one-dimensional marginals are Bernoulli$(1/2)$.
2. Determine the joint pmf and the sum law in both models.
3. Determine which, if either, of the two pairs is independent.
4. Let $h(x,y)=\mathbf1_{\{x+y\ge1\}}$. Compute the law and expectation of $h(X_+,Y_+)$ and $h(X_-,Y_-)$. Identify the measure being pushed forward in each calculation.
5. State the strongest conclusion about $X+Y$ which can be obtained from Bernoulli$(1/2)$ marginals alone.

Limit: 15 minutes after the canonical TD attempt.

## Exercise B — Finite-population correction in a numerical model

A population contains $N=20$ objects, of which $K=7$ are successes. A sample of size $n=5$ is drawn uniformly without replacement, and $H$ denotes the number of sampled successes.

1. State the exact support and pmf of $H$.
2. Construct $H$ from ordered-draw indicators and compute $\mathbb E[H]$.
3. Compute $\operatorname{Var}(H)$, first in structural form and then as an exact rational number.
4. Compare this variance with that of a binomial$(5,7/20)$ variable. Identify the covariance term which accounts for the difference.
5. State what happens to the variance formula if $n=N$, and separately explain why the displayed quotient is not used when $N=1$.

Limit: 15 minutes after the canonical TD attempt.

## Exercise C — Finite and infinite waiting structures

A finite sequence of $N=12$ positions contains exactly $K=5$ successes, and the success set is uniform among the $5$-subsets of $\{1,\ldots,12\}$. Let $T^{(12)}_2$ be the position of the second success.

1. Determine the exact support of $T^{(12)}_2$.
2. Derive its pmf from the number of admissible success sets.
3. Compare it with the trial-convention negative-binomial variable $T_2$ generated by iid Bernoulli$(5/12)$ trials. Give two structural reasons why the laws are different.
4. For the iid model, prove

   $$
   \{T_2\le n\}
   =
   \{\operatorname{Bin}(n,5/12)\ge2\}.
   $$

5. Convert $T_2$ to the failures-before-second-success convention and state the new support. Treat the endpoints $p=0$ and $p=1$ separately.

Limit: 15 minutes after the canonical TD attempt.

## Exercise D — One Poisson model through three representations

Let $N\sim\operatorname{Pois}(4)$. Conditional on $N=n$, independently label every event A with probability $1/3$ and B with probability $2/3$. Let $K$ and $L$ be the type counts.

1. Derive the joint pmf of $(K,L)$ and factorize it.
2. Identify the marginal laws and prove that $K$ and $L$ are independent.
3. Determine the conditional law of $K$ given $K+L=5$.
4. For

   $$
   Y=\frac{K(K+1)}2,
   $$

   compute $\mathbb E[Y]$ using factorial moments.
5. Compute $g(\mathbb E[K])$ for $g(k)=k(k+1)/2$ and identify the missing term.
6. State exactly which conclusions fail if the type labels are no longer conditionally independent.

Limit: 15 minutes after the canonical TD attempt.

## Exercise E — Mixture and truncation are different operations

Let $I\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(1/4)$ and, conditionally on $I=i$, let

$$
N\mid I=0\sim\operatorname{Pois}(1),
\qquad
N\mid I=1\sim\operatorname{Pois}(4).
$$

1. Derive the unconditional pmf of $N$.
2. Compute $\mathbb E[N]$ and $\operatorname{Var}(N)$ without summing the mixture pmf term by term.
3. Prove that the unconditional law is not Poisson.
4. Compute $\mathbb P(I=1\mid N=0)$, naming the restricted masses before normalizing.
5. Independently, let $M\sim\operatorname{Pois}(\lambda)$ with $\lambda>0$. Derive the pmf, mean and variance of $M$ conditional on $M\ge1$.
6. Explain why the mixture in Questions 1–4 and the truncation in Question 5 both retain Poisson factors in their numerators but are produced by different operations on measures.

Limit: 20 minutes after the canonical TD attempt.

## Closed-book reconstruction — Sixty minutes

This reconstruction is a mastery extension and may be completed after the $810$-minute core.

1. Prove the atomic representation theorem and state the uncountable counting-measure counterexample.
2. Derive the fibre formula and the law of a sum as pushforwards.
3. Give identical marginals with different sum laws and locate the missing product-law hypothesis.
4. Construct binomial and hypergeometric laws from their probability spaces; derive the finite-population correction.
5. Derive geometric and negative-binomial waiting laws and state both conventions.
6. Derive Poisson factorial moments, superposition, splitting and conditional allocation without an MGF.
7. Separate normalized restriction, mixture and posterior reversal.
8. Complete the integrated chain

   $$
   \text{latent class}
   \to
   \text{conditional count}
   \to
   \text{thinning}
   \to
   \text{mixture}
   \to
   \text{nonlinear payment}.
   $$
