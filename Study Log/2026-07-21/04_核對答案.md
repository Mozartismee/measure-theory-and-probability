## Exercise 2

Applying the canonical marginalization result to the triangular density gives

$$
\int_0^1\int_0^x2\,dz\,dx=1,
$$

$$
f_X(x)=2x\mathbf1_{(0,1)}(x),
\qquad
f_Z(z)=2(1-z)\mathbf1_{(0,1)}(z).
$$

For bounded signed $\varphi$, the test identity follows from the canonical pushforward identity in Product Measures TD Exercise 2(3), after decomposing $\varphi$ into positive and negative parts. ## Exercise 3

The inverse transformation is

$$
(v,u)\longmapsto(x,z)=(v,uv),
$$

with absolute Jacobian $v$. Therefore

$$
f_{V,U}(v,u)
=
2v\mathbf1_{(0,1)^2}(v,u)
=
\bigl(2v\mathbf1_{(0,1)}(v)\bigr)
\bigl(\mathbf1_{(0,1)}(u)\bigr).
$$

Thus $V$ and $U$ are independent, $V$ has density $2v\mathbf1_{(0,1)}$, and $U$ is uniform on $(0,1)$. The identity $Z=UX$ realizes the conditional uniform scaling explicitly.

## Exercise 4

On $(\Omega,\mathcal G)$,

$$
\nu_Y(A)=\mathbb E[Y\mathbf1_A],
\qquad
\mathbb E[Y\mid\mathcal G]
=
\frac{d\nu_Y}{d(\mathbb P|_{\mathcal G})}.
$$

Here $\nu_Y$ is finite signed and absolutely continuous with respect to $\mathbb P|_{\mathcal G}$; its derivative is unique $\mathbb P$-almost surely.

On the state space,

$$
\rho_Y(B)=\mathbb E[Y\mathbf1_{\{X\in B\}}],
\qquad
g_Y=\frac{d\rho_Y}{d\mu_X},
$$

where $\rho_Y$ is finite signed and $\rho_Y\ll\mu_X$. Thus $g_Y$ is unique $\mu_X$-almost everywhere and $g_Y(X)=\mathbb E[Y\mid\sigma(X)]$. For every bounded measurable $\varphi:S\to\mathbb R$,

$$
\mathbb E[Y\varphi(X)]
=
\int\varphi g_Y\,d\mu_X
=
\mathbb E[g_Y(X)\varphi(X)].
$$

For an event $D$ with $\mathbb P(D)>0$,

$$
\frac{d\mathbb P_D}{d\mathbb P}
=
\frac{\mathbf1_D}{\mathbb P(D)},
\qquad
\mathbb P(D)\frac{d\mu_D}{d\mu_X}
=
g_D,
$$

where $g_D(X)=\mathbb E[\mathbf1_D\mid\sigma(X)]$. The first derivative lives on $\Omega$; the second lives on the state space of $X$.

## Exercise 5

The finite-partition formula is

$$
\mathbb E[Y\mid\mathcal G]
=
\sum_i\frac{\mathbb E[Y\mathbf1_{B_i}]}{\mathbb P(B_i)}\mathbf1_{B_i}.
$$

The tower identity follows by taking expectations. Set $H=\mathbb E[Y\mid\mathcal G]-\mathbb E[Y]\in L^2(\mathcal G)$. The defining test identity gives the zero cross term first for bounded truncations of $H$; Cauchy–Schwarz and $L^2$ convergence pass the identity to $H$. Expanding the square yields

$$
\operatorname{Var}(Y)
=
\mathbb E[\operatorname{Var}(Y\mid\mathcal G)]
+
\operatorname{Var}(\mathbb E[Y\mid\mathcal G]).
$$

The $L^2$ hypothesis guarantees integrability of every square and cross product.

## Exercise 6

Here $D_X=(0,1)$ and

$$
k(x,z)
=
\frac1x\mathbf1_{(0,x)}(z),
\qquad x\in(0,1).
$$

Complete it elsewhere with any fixed density $q$. For Borel $A,B$,

$$
\int_AK(x,B)f_X(x)\,dx
=
\int_A\int_Bf(x,z)\,dz\,dx,
$$

which identifies $K(X,B)$ by conditional-expectation uniqueness. Moreover, for $x\in(0,1)$,

$$
\int_0^xz\frac{dz}{x}=\frac x2,
$$

so $\mathbb E[Z\mid\sigma(X)]=X/2$ almost surely. Complete $U=Z/X$ arbitrarily on $\{X=0\}$. Its conditional law given $X=x$ is uniform on $(0,1)$ for $\mu_X$-almost every $x$ and is independent of $x$, hence $U$ and $X$ are independent.

## Exercise 7

The mixture density is

$$
f_X(x)
=
\left((1-p)\lambda_0e^{-\lambda_0x}
+p\lambda_1e^{-\lambda_1x}\right)
\mathbf1_{(0,+\infty)}(x).
$$

On $x>0$,

$$
r_1(x)
=
\frac{p\lambda_1e^{-\lambda_1x}}
{(1-p)\lambda_0e^{-\lambda_0x}+p\lambda_1e^{-\lambda_1x}}
$$

is a posterior version; set $r_1(x)=p$ on $x\le0$. The numerator measure is $p\mu_1$, the reference measure is the mixture law $\mu_X$, and

$$
\mathbb E[\lambda_I\mid\sigma(X)]
=
\lambda_0(1-r_1(X))+\lambda_1r_1(X).
$$

Finite Bayes normalizes joint masses; density Bayes normalizes the corresponding joint measure densities.
