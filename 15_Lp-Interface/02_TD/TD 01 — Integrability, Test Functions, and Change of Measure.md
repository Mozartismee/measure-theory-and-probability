# TD 01 — Integrability, Test Functions, and Change of Measure

Let $(E,\mathcal A,\mu)$ be a measure space. The purpose of this TD is not to rebuild the whole theory of Banach spaces. It isolates the $L^p$ interfaces later used by signed measures, Radon–Nikodym theory and conditional expectation.

Hölder's inequality and the standard convergence theorems may be used.

## Exercise 1 — Finite-measure embeddings and their boundary

1. Assume that $\mu(E)<+\infty$. Let

   $$
   1\le p<q<+\infty.
   $$

   Prove that

   $$
   L^q(\mu)\subseteq L^p(\mu)
   $$

   and establish the exact norm estimate.

2. Treat the case $q=+\infty$.

3. On $(\mathbb R,\mathcal B(\mathbb R),\lambda)$, construct:

   - a function in $L^1(\lambda)$ which belongs to no $L^p(\lambda)$ with $p>1$;
   - for each fixed $p>1$, a function in $L^p(\lambda)$ which does not belong to $L^1(\lambda)$.

4. State precisely which hypothesis is responsible for the chain

   $$
   L^\infty(\mu)
   \subseteq
   L^q(\mu)
   \subseteq
   L^p(\mu)
   \subseteq
   L^1(\mu).
   $$

## Exercise 2 — Indicators and bounded test functions

Let $f,g\in L^1(\mu)$ and let $\mathcal G\subseteq\mathcal A$ be a sub-$\sigma$-field.

1. Prove that for every $Z\in L^\infty(\mu)$,

   $$
   fZ\in L^1(\mu),
   \qquad
   \|fZ\|_1\le \|Z\|_\infty\|f\|_1.
   $$

2. Assume that

   $$
   \int_A f\,d\mu
   =
   \int_A g\,d\mu,
   \qquad A\in\mathcal G.
   $$

   Prove that

   $$
   \int_E Zf\,d\mu
   =
   \int_E Zg\,d\mu
   $$

   for every bounded $\mathcal G$-measurable real function $Z$.

3. Explain why the appropriate passage from simple functions to bounded measurable functions uses the dominated convergence theorem rather than the monotone convergence theorem.

4. Assume in addition that $f$ and $g$ are $\mathcal G$-measurable. Prove that

   $$
   f=g
   \qquad\mu\text{-almost everywhere}.
   $$

## Exercise 3 — Measures generated by densities

Let $h:E\to[0,+\infty]$ be measurable.

1. Prove that

   $$
   (h\mu)(A)=\int_A h\,d\mu,
   \qquad A\in\mathcal A,
   $$

   defines a positive measure and that

   $$
   h\mu\ll\mu.
   $$

2. Determine when $h\mu$ is finite.

3. Let $f\in L^1(\mu)$. Prove that

   $$
   \nu_f(A)=\int_A f\,d\mu
   $$

   defines a finite signed measure.

4. With

   $$
   P=\{f\ge0\},
   \qquad
   N=\{f<0\},
   $$

   identify the Hahn decomposition, Jordan decomposition and total variation of $\nu_f$.

5. Let $f,g\in L^1(\mu)$. Prove that

   $$
   f\mu=g\mu
   \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
   f=g
   \quad\mu\text{-almost everywhere}.
   $$

## Exercise 4 — Change of measure and equivalence classes

Let $\nu$ be a positive measure such that

$$
\nu=h\mu
$$

for some measurable $h\ge0$.

1. Prove that for every measurable $f$,

   $$
   f\in L^1(\nu)
   \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
   \int_E |f|h\,d\mu<+\infty,
   $$

   and then

   $$
   \int_E f\,d\nu
   =
   \int_E fh\,d\mu.
   $$

2. Assume that $h\le C$ $\mu$-almost everywhere. Prove that the canonical rule

   $$
   T_{\mu\to\nu}:L^1(\mu)\longrightarrow L^1(\nu),
   \qquad
   [f]_\mu\longmapsto[f]_\nu,
   $$

   is well defined and bounded, and give the norm estimate. Must it be injective?

3. Give an example in which $\nu\ll\mu$ and both measures are finite, but integrability does not transfer from $L^1(\mu)$ to $L^1(\nu)$.

4. Separate the two assertions required for the map

   $$
   [f]_\mu\longmapsto[f]_\nu
   $$

   to define a map on all of $L^1(\mu)$: integrability transfer and compatibility of equivalence classes. Then state the additional null-set relation that makes this map injective.

5. Prove that if $h>0$ $\mu$-almost everywhere, then $\mu$ and $\nu$ have the same null sets.

## Reconstruction — The interface to retain

Without consulting the Cours, reconstruct the following chain and justify every arrow:

$$
f\in L^1(\mu),\quad Z\in L^\infty(\mu)
\Longrightarrow
fZ\in L^1(\mu),
$$

$$
\text{indicator tests}
\Longrightarrow
\text{bounded measurable tests},
$$

$$
f\in L^1(\mu)
\Longrightarrow
f\mu\text{ is a finite signed measure},
$$

$$
f\mu=g\mu
\Longrightarrow
f=g\quad\mu\text{-a.e.}
$$

Close by explaining why $\nu\ll\mu$ controls null sets but does not, by itself, control the transfer of integrability.
