# Cours — Construction of the Radon–Nikodym Derivative

This course depends on [[10_Foundations/Signed Measures and Hahn-Jordan Decomposition|signed measures and Hahn decomposition]]. The proof route is Hahn-first; the $L^2$ route in Le Gall is comparative, not implicit.

## 1. Absolute continuity

Let $(E,\mathcal A)$ be a measurable space and let $\mu,\nu$ be positive measures on it.

### Definition

One writes

$$
\nu\ll\mu
$$

if

$$
\mu(A)=0\Longrightarrow \nu(A)=0,
\qquad A\in\mathcal A.
$$

Absolute continuity is a statement about null sets. It is not, by itself, a representation theorem.

## 2. Measures generated by densities

Let $f:E\to[0,+\infty]$ be measurable. Define

$$
(f\mu)(A)=\int_A f\,d\mu,
\qquad A\in\mathcal A.
$$

Then $f\mu$ is a positive measure and

$$
f\mu\ll\mu.
$$

Thus

$$
\nu=f\mu\Longrightarrow \nu\ll\mu
$$

without any finiteness assumption. The converse is the content of the Radon–Nikodym theorem and requires an admissible measure-theoretic regime.

## 3. The Radon–Nikodym theorem

### Theorem

Assume that $\mu$ and $\nu$ are $\sigma$-finite positive measures on $(E,\mathcal A)$ and that

$$
\nu\ll\mu.
$$

There exists a measurable function $f:E\to[0,+\infty)$ such that

$$
\nu(A)=\int_A f\,d\mu,
\qquad A\in\mathcal A.
$$

The function $f$ is unique $\mu$-almost everywhere. It is denoted by

$$
f=\frac{d\nu}{d\mu}.
$$

For every nonnegative measurable function $\varphi$,

$$
\int_E\varphi\,d\nu
=
\int_E\varphi\frac{d\nu}{d\mu}\,d\mu.
$$

The same identity holds for every $\varphi\in L^1(\nu)$; in that case

$$
\varphi\frac{d\nu}{d\mu}\in L^1(\mu).
$$

The proof is first carried out for finite measures. The $\sigma$-finite theorem is then obtained by dévissage.

## 4. Hahn decomposition

Let $\sigma$ be a finite signed measure on $(E,\mathcal A)$.

A measurable set $P$ is positive for $\sigma$ if

$$
\sigma(A)\ge 0
$$

for every measurable $A\subset P$. A measurable set $N$ is negative for $\sigma$ if

$$
\sigma(A)\le 0
$$

for every measurable $A\subset N$.

### Hahn decomposition theorem

There exists a measurable partition

$$
E=P\sqcup N
$$

such that $P$ is positive and $N$ is negative for $\sigma$.

Only this theorem is used below. No Jordan decomposition is required.

## 5. Local density fragment

### Lemma

Let $\mu$ and $\rho$ be finite positive measures such that

$$
0\ne\rho\ll\mu.
$$

There exist $\varepsilon>0$ and $P\in\mathcal A$ such that

$$
\mu(P)>0
$$

and

$$
\varepsilon\mathbf 1_P\mu\le\rho.
$$

Equivalently,

$$
\varepsilon\mu(A\cap P)\le\rho(A),
\qquad A\in\mathcal A.
$$

### Demonstration

Choose $A\in\mathcal A$ such that $\rho(A)>0$. Since $\mu$ is finite, one may choose $n\ge1$ such that

$$
\rho(A)-\frac1n\mu(A)>0.
$$

Set

$$
\sigma_n=\rho-\frac1n\mu
$$

and let

$$
E=P\sqcup N
$$

be a Hahn decomposition for $\sigma_n$. Since $\sigma_n(A)>0$, one has $\sigma_n(P)>0$. Hence $\mu(P)>0$: otherwise $\rho(P)=0$ by $\rho\ll\mu$, and therefore $\sigma_n(P)=0$.

For every measurable $B\subset P$,

$$
0\le\sigma_n(B)
=
\rho(B)-\frac1n\mu(B).
$$

Thus

$$
\frac1n\mathbf 1_P\mu\le\rho.
$$

Take $\varepsilon=1/n$.

## 6. Representable submeasures

Assume from now on that $\mu$ and $\nu$ are finite positive measures and that

$$
\nu\ll\mu.
$$

Define

$$
\mathcal C
=
\left\{
h:E\to[0,+\infty]\text{ measurable}:
h\mu\le\nu
\right\}.
$$

The elements of $\mathcal C$ parameterize the submeasures of $\nu$ representable by a density with respect to $\mu$.

### Proposition

The class $\mathcal C$ is nonempty and stable under finite maxima.

### Demonstration

Clearly $0\in\mathcal C$. Let $f,g\in\mathcal C$. For $A\in\mathcal A$,

$$
\int_A(f\vee g)\,d\mu
=
\int_{A\cap\{f\ge g\}}f\,d\mu
+
\int_{A\cap\{f<g\}}g\,d\mu.
$$

Therefore

$$
\int_A(f\vee g)\,d\mu
\le
\nu(A\cap\{f\ge g\})
+
\nu(A\cap\{f<g\})
=
\nu(A).
$$

Hence $f\vee g\in\mathcal C$.

## 7. Maximal represented mass

Set

$$
\alpha
=
\sup_{h\in\mathcal C}
\int_E h\,d\mu.
$$

Since $h\mu\le\nu$ for every $h\in\mathcal C$,

$$
0\le\alpha\le\nu(E)<+\infty.
$$

Choose $h_n\in\mathcal C$ such that

$$
\int_E h_n\,d\mu\longrightarrow\alpha.
$$

Define

$$
g_n=h_1\vee\cdots\vee h_n,
\qquad
g=\sup_{n\ge1}g_n.
$$

Then $g_n\in\mathcal C$ and $g_n\uparrow g$. For every $A\in\mathcal A$, the monotone convergence theorem gives

$$
\int_A g\,d\mu
=
\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_A g_n\,d\mu
\le
\nu(A).
$$

Thus $g\in\mathcal C$. Moreover,

$$
\int_E g\,d\mu=\alpha.
$$

In particular, $g<+\infty$ $\mu$-almost everywhere.

## 8. Elimination of the residue

Define

$$
\rho=\nu-g\mu.
$$

Since $g\mu\le\nu$, the set function $\rho$ is a finite positive measure. Moreover,

$$
\rho\ll\mu.
$$

Assume that $\rho\ne0$. By the local density fragment lemma, there exist $\varepsilon>0$ and $P\in\mathcal A$ such that

$$
\mu(P)>0,
\qquad
\varepsilon\mathbf1_P\mu\le\rho.
$$

Set

$$
h=g+\varepsilon\mathbf1_P.
$$

For every $A\in\mathcal A$,

$$
\int_Ah\,d\mu
=
\int_Ag\,d\mu
+
\varepsilon\mu(A\cap P)
\le
g\mu(A)+\rho(A)
=
\nu(A).
$$

Hence $h\in\mathcal C$. But

$$
\int_Eh\,d\mu
=
\alpha+\varepsilon\mu(P)
>
\alpha,
$$

contradicting the definition of $\alpha$. Therefore

$$
\rho=0,
$$

and consequently

$$
\nu=g\mu.
$$

This proves existence in the finite case.

## 9. Uniqueness in the finite case

Let $f,g:E\to[0,+\infty]$ be measurable and assume that

$$
f\mu=g\mu=\nu,
$$

where $\nu$ is finite. For $n\ge1$, set

$$
A_n=\left\{f\ge g+\frac1n\right\}.
$$

Since

$$
\int_{A_n}f\,d\mu
=
\int_{A_n}g\,d\mu
<+\infty,
$$

while $f\ge g+1/n$ on $A_n$, one obtains

$$
\int_{A_n}g\,d\mu
+
\frac1n\mu(A_n)
\le
\int_{A_n}f\,d\mu
=
\int_{A_n}g\,d\mu.
$$

Thus $\mu(A_n)=0$ for every $n$, hence $f\le g$ $\mu$-almost everywhere. By symmetry,

$$
f=g
\qquad\mu\text{-almost everywhere}.
$$

The finiteness of the common represented measure prevents the undefined subtraction $+\infty-+\infty$.

## 10. The $\sigma$-finite case

Assume that $\mu$ and $\nu$ are $\sigma$-finite and that $\nu\ll\mu$.

Choose measurable covers

$$
E=\bigcup_{m\ge1}A_m
=
\bigcup_{n\ge1}B_n
$$

such that

$$
\mu(A_m)<+\infty,
\qquad
\nu(B_n)<+\infty.
$$

Enumerate the family $(A_m\cap B_n)_{m,n\ge1}$ as $(C_k)_{k\ge1}$ and define

$$
E_1=C_1,
\qquad
E_k=C_k\setminus\bigcup_{j<k}C_j
\quad(k\ge2).
$$

Then

$$
E=\bigsqcup_{k\ge1}E_k,
\qquad
\mu(E_k)<+\infty,
\qquad
\nu(E_k)<+\infty.
$$

On $(E_k,\mathcal A|_{E_k})$, the finite theorem gives a measurable $f_k\ge0$ such that

$$
\nu(A)=\int_Af_k\,d\mu
$$

for every measurable $A\subset E_k$. Define

$$
f=\sum_{k\ge1}f_k\mathbf1_{E_k}.
$$

For every $A\in\mathcal A$,

$$
\nu(A)
=
\sum_{k\ge1}\nu(A\cap E_k)
=
\sum_{k\ge1}\int_{A\cap E_k}f_k\,d\mu
=
\int_Af\,d\mu.
$$

Uniqueness follows by restriction to each $E_k$.

## 11. Boundary of the theorem

Let $\mu$ be counting measure on $([0,1],\mathcal B([0,1]))$ and let $\lambda$ be Lebesgue measure. Then

$$
\lambda\ll\mu,
$$

but there is no measurable $f\ge0$ such that

$$
\lambda=f\mu.
$$

The measure $\mu$ is not $\sigma$-finite. Absolute continuity alone therefore does not imply representability by a density.

## 12. Reconstruction

Reconstruct the proof without consulting the preceding sections.

### Finite regime

$$
\mathcal C
\longrightarrow
\vee
\longrightarrow
(g_n)
\longrightarrow
g
\longrightarrow
\rho=\nu-g\mu
\longrightarrow
\varepsilon\mathbf1_P\mu
\longrightarrow
\rho=0.
$$

The admissible objects must arise in this order:

1. representable submeasures $h\mu\le\nu$;
2. stability under finite maxima;
3. maximization of represented mass;
4. monotone passage to the candidate density;
5. residual positive measure;
6. local density fragment obtained from Hahn decomposition;
7. contradiction by strict enlargement.

### Global regime

$$
\sigma\text{-finite covers}
\longrightarrow
\text{common disjoint partition}
\longrightarrow
\text{finite derivatives}
\longrightarrow
\text{gluing}
\longrightarrow
\text{local uniqueness}.
$$
